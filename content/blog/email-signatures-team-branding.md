A ten-person team sends somewhere between 5,000 and 10,000 emails a month. Every one of those emails ends with a signature — and at most companies, those signatures are a mess: different fonts, outdated job titles, broken logos on mobile, and the occasional Comic Sans.

That's thousands of missed brand impressions a month, going to the exact people who already open your emails.

## Why signatures break

Email signatures look simple but are genuinely hard to get right, because email clients render HTML differently:

- **Gmail strips certain styles** and has its own image quirks.
- **Outlook renders HTML with Word's engine**, which breaks modern CSS layouts.
- **Apple Mail and mobile clients** scale images unpredictably.

A signature that looks perfect in one client can fall apart in another. Multiply that by every employee copy-pasting and "fixing" their own signature, and brand consistency is gone within a month.

## What a good signature setup looks like

From building [Emailify](/products/emailify), our email signature platform, we've settled on a few principles:

**Design once, centrally.** Signatures should come from one template controlled by whoever owns your brand — not from each employee's settings page. When the logo or tagline changes, it should change everywhere at once.

**Test across real clients.** A signature must render pixel-perfect in Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail before anyone uses it. Table-based layouts and hosted images are still the reliable way to do that.

**Keep it scannable.** Name, role, company, one or two contact methods, and a single call-to-action link. Signatures with six icons and three banners get ignored.

**Measure it.** If your signature links to your website, a product page, or a booking link, you should know whether anyone clicks. Click tracking turns the signature from decoration into a measurable channel.

## Where the ROI shows up

Companies that treat signatures as a channel use that space deliberately: promoting a product launch for a quarter, linking a case study for the sales team, or pointing support emails at a help center. Because the audience is already engaged — they're reading your email — click-through rates on signature CTAs are consistently respectable for a channel that costs nothing per send.
