Every small business we've worked with runs on the same fragile setup: a spreadsheet for inventory, another for expenses, an accounting tool nobody fully understands, and a group chat where sales are reported. Each tool works — until they need to agree with each other.

That gap is why we built [ORBS](/products/orbs), our all-in-one business management platform.

## The problem with disconnected tools

When your inventory lives in one place and your sales in another, every question becomes a reconciliation project:

- *How much did we actually profit last month?* Cross-reference three spreadsheets.
- *Can we fulfill this order?* Check the stock sheet — and hope someone updated it.
- *Who changed this number?* Nobody knows.

The cost isn't just time. It's decisions made on stale or contradictory data, and team members with access to everything because fine-grained permissions were too hard to set up.

## What ORBS does differently

ORBS starts from a simple idea: **one source of truth**. Sales, purchases, inventory, accounting, and team management share the same data, so a sale automatically affects stock levels and the ledger — no copy-pasting between systems.

The pieces that matter most to our users:

### Role-based team management

Owners define roles and decide exactly which modules each person can see and edit. Your accountant sees the books; your warehouse staff sees inventory; nobody sees more than they need.

### Accounting that stays in sync

Income and expenses are recorded against real transactions — purchases and sales that already exist in the system — so the books reconcile themselves as you work.

### Inventory you can trust

Stock levels update as purchases arrive and sales go out. When someone asks "can we fulfill this?", the answer is on the dashboard, not in someone's head.

### A dashboard that answers questions

Instead of assembling reports from scraps, the ORBS dashboard shows sales, purchases, stock, and team activity at a glance.

## Built the way we build for clients

ORBS runs on the same stack we use for client work — a modern web application with a robust API backend — and it's shaped by the same process: plan, build, test with real users, refine. Running our own products keeps us honest about what it takes to maintain software long after launch.
